Computer Graphics

Outside of my work in linguistics, I like to use Computer Graphics as a creative outlet.
The piece showcased here was made in open source software Blender. More pieces I have made can be found here.

Also known as 3D Modeling or Computer Generated Imagery (CGI), Computer Graphics isn't an art form in the traditional sense. Its roots are in technical modeling, diagrams, and manufacturing. In 1977's Star Wars: A New Hope, for example, computer generated models were only used for an in-universe technical demonstration before the trench run. This changed through the late 90s and early 2000s as companies like Pixar used CGI for creative storytelling.

My work tends to blend creative expression with the medium itself. Below is a video of a 3D model of a lightbulb. Instead of using traditional rendering practices, an intentionally reduced samples per pixel count has been used to create a noisy image. The noisy image is then run through an algorithmic denoiser which tries to fill in the gaps. The denoiser, working with insufficient data, is imperfect. This has a similar effect to hand-painted watercolors. Big areas get similar colors and gradients. Sharp contrasts preserve details around edges, soft gradients blur details away from edges. Unlike watercolors, since this is being done by a computer, it can be done several times from different angles. This creates the warping effect as the bulb spins. This, and other pieces like it, show my computer science knowledge. They also show a willingness to engage with technical subjects with an artistic mindset.
